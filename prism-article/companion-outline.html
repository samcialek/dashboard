how light refracts

march 2026 · companion to "the cause of our politics"
"Liberal" and "conservative" are not fundamental forces — they are principal components. Like PCA in statistics, they compress a high-dimensional disposition space into convenient axes that capture the most variance in a given era. But principal components are artifacts of the decomposition, not the underlying reality. They rotate when the data changes. The thing underneath — the actual multi-dimensional state space of political disposition — doesn't rotate. PRISM maps that state space.

framing I. "liberal" and "conservative" are principal components, not causes

"Liberal" and "conservative" are the names we give to the first two principal components of political behavior in a given era. They are not real forces. They are summary statistics.
  • PCA analogy: "left-right" is PC1 of American politics. "Libertarian-authoritarian" is PC2. They feel real because they explain a lot. But they're projections, not primitives.
  • The components rotate across eras. What "liberal" captures in 1972 (Great Society, labor unions, anti-war) is not what it captures in 2024 (identity politics, credentialism, technocratic governance). Same label, different loading vector.
  • If you build a model on the components, it breaks every time the political landscape rearranges. If you build on the underlying dimensions, it survives. That's the difference between descriptive and generative.

L1 II. temperament: the priors you were born with

What it is: Cognitive style, risk tolerance, time horizon, openness, comfort with ambiguity, need for order, universalism vs. parochialism. The hardware.

Key insight: L1 defines a range of possible political dispositions, not a single one. It sets the boundaries; L2 selects within them.

justification for L1 priors selected

  • Conflict orientation — how much you seek or avoid confrontation. Downstream of Big Five Agreeableness but more specific. Determines whether politics feels like a battle to win or a problem to solve.
  • Rule orientation — comfort with explicit structure vs. contextual judgment. A rule-oriented universalist produces very different politics than a rule-oriented tribalist.
  • Abstraction tolerance — willingness to reason about systems vs. concrete cases. Predicts whether someone engages with policy as mechanism design or as moral narrative.
  • Ambiguity tolerance — capacity to hold uncertainty without resolving it prematurely. Low ambiguity tolerance + high threat sensitivity = very specific political phenotype.
  • Time horizon — how far forward you optimize. Short time horizons favor concrete, immediate policy; long favor systemic, structural thinking.
  • Universalism vs. parochialism — the radius of your moral circle. The single most consequential L1 dimension for predicting coalition membership.

Bayesian framing: L1 priors are literally Bayesian priors. Given someone's expressed politics, what are the possible L1 configurations that could have produced them? PRISM inverts the generative process.

L2 III. formative context: the prism itself

What it is: Where you grew up, class position, education, religion, region, ethnic status, economic volatility, media exposure, institutional trust, direct experience with disorder or exclusion.

transformation: L1 → L2 (endogenous meets exogenous)
  • White light (L1 temperament) enters a prism (L2 formative context) and separates into a spectrum (L3 disposition). Same light through a different prism = different spectrum. But the range is the same.
  • L2 doesn't override L1 — it selects from the possibility space that L1 defines.
  • The nationalism test: would a MAGA nationalist of the JD Vance variety be the same kind of nationalist if born into the majority religion/ethnicity of a different country? If yes, L1 dominates. If no, L2 dominates. PRISM says: some of each, and the ratio is measurable.
"I Know This About Myself, I Assume as Much for Other People" — the title for the declared vs. revealed preferences section.
  • Self-report introduces systematic bias. One way to get at revealed preferences: have someone else answer the questions about you.
  • The quiz triangulates revealed preferences by asking questions whose political valence isn't immediately obvious.

L3 IV. political disposition: the spectrum after refraction

transformation: L2 → L3 (selection from possibility space)
  • Not a point on a left-right line, but a position in a multi-dimensional space mapping to one of the archetypes.
  • The 10 disposition dimensions emerge here: markets, coercion, hierarchy, pluralism, national identity, moral traditionalism, elite stewardship, zero-sum conflict, social change, etc.
  • Someone can score high on public order and low on economic hierarchy, or high on pluralism and high on technocratic control — real politics contains these cross-currents.
  • "Liberal" and "conservative" are lossy compressions — they capture the dominant axis of a given era's coalitional sorting but erase the cross-pressures.

why ~132 archetypes and not 2

  • Two archetypes = PCA with k=2. You've thrown away most of the information.
  • The archetype count is empirical — it's the resolution at which prediction stops improving.

L4 V. current context: the temporal overlay

What it is: Economic shocks, war, migration, elite failure, technological change, charismatic candidates, institutional decay, crime waves, perceived humiliation, party sorting.

transformation: L3 → L4 (reinforcement learning connection)
  • L4 doesn't change who you are (L1-L3). It changes which parts of who you are become salient.
  • Maps to reinforcement learning: L3 disposition = policy function. L4 = environment/reward signal. Same policy function, different actions under different conditions.
  • Explore vs. exploit: L1 (openness, ambiguity tolerance) sets the explore/exploit ratio. L4 (how threatening the environment is) shifts it. Under threat, exploit dominates — people retreat to coalition defaults.
  • This is how realignment happens: not by people becoming fundamentally different, but by different parts of their latent structure becoming activated.

the endogenous-exogenous handoff

  • L1-L3 = endogenous (internal, relatively stable). L4 = exogenous (external, changing).
  • Pre-compute all combinations of political behaviors for each archetype across L4 conditions. Master lookup instead of runtime computation.
  • Connection to Aaru: archetypes are cluster centers, L4 dynamics are the environmental simulation layer.

L5 VI. political behavior: the observable output

transformation: L4 → L5 (activation → action)
  • Votes, issue positions, party affiliation, candidate support, movement participation, abstention. The thing everyone measures.
  • L5 is where most models start and stop. Decent snapshot, poor transfer.
  • The same L5 can be generated by completely different L1-L4 configurations — the overdetermination problem.
  • "Two voters, one survey score" — same output, different generators, different futures.

meta VII. concept drift and the three-body problem

Politics exhibits massive concept drift. Three forces act on each other — a genuine three-body problem.
  • The three forces: (1) elite positioning, (2) mass sentiment, (3) institutional/media framing.
  • Each influences the other two, making equilibria temporary.
  • Simple models assume stationarity. PRISM assumes non-stationarity and models the drift mechanism.
  • This is why "liberal" rotates: the coalitional sorting algorithm changed what the first principal component captures.

meta VIII. applications

  • Coalition simulation: Not who wins, but who aligns with whom under different conditions.
  • The Politracker: Put your map against someone else's and find actual overlap vs. assumed opposition.
  • Historical extension: Apply PRISM to the Founding Fathers — what were their L1s?
  • Expansion packs: Modular additions for going deeper on specific policy areas.
