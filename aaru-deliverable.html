01 Executive Summary

14 models. 74,000 responses. One finding.

We trained 14 models — spanning linear regression, tree ensembles, neural networks, and variational autoencoders — on 74,000 harmonized survey responses to predict 58 psychographic attitudes from 15 demographic features.

The prediction ceiling is R² ≈ 0.20. No architecture breaks through it. XGBoost hits 0.218; the Causal VAE hits 0.202. The difference is noise. What's not noise is what each model can do with its predictions.

15 demographic features contain approximately 20% of the variance in psychographic attitudes. The remaining 80% comes from life experiences, social networks, media consumption, and idiosyncratic personality. This isn't a failure — it's a measurement of the information boundary between who you are (demographics) and what you believe (psychographics).

The question isn't "can we predict better?" — the ceiling is the ceiling. The question is: what can you do with your predictions?

04 GLP-1 Client Scenario

Joint probability says −17%. Causal simulation says −7.6%. Here's why.

GLP-1 Impact on Alcohol Consumption: Two Models Diverge
10,000 synthetic agents · 24-month simulation · baseline avg 9.74 drinks/month
Joint Probability (linear extrapolation)
Causal Simulation (with second-order effects)

Joint Probability Model

Sees: GLP-1 users drink less → extrapolates linearly with adoption. Predicts a steady decline to −17.25% at month 24. No mechanism for reversal — it's a correlation lookup, not a causal model.

Causal Simulation

Traces the pathway: GLP-1 → weight loss → confidence → sociality → more drinking occasions. Per-occasion volume drops (2.65 → 1.68 drinks), but occasions nearly double (3.3 → 5.6/month). Net: decline flattens at −9.4% then reverses to −7.6%.

The 9.6 percentage point gap between these models is the difference between a snapshot and a movie. The causal simulation captures the second-order social confidence effect that the joint probability model structurally cannot see.

From causal profiles to dynamic agent behavior

Three Response Mechanisms

Direct Intervention

do-calculus: set target dims, propagate through DAG structural equations, decode to behavior.

Social Contagion

Agent latent state pulled toward neighbors via contact network. Demographic dims frozen; opinion dims drift.

Temporal Evolution

Natural drift: age-related changes + mean reversion on opinion dimensions.

06 RL Architecture Roadmap

From contextual bandits to mean field RL — phased for a 24-person team

The core upgrade: Static inference gives you P(behavior | traits) — a photograph. RL gives you π(aₜ | sₜ, z) where state evolves via learned dynamics — a movie where you can change the script mid-scene.

Four-Phase Implementation

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4
Contextual Bandits

Bandit policy over Causal VAE profiles + context → action distributions. Offline training from historical data. A/B comparison vs. joint probability on 3+ scenarios. Team: 2–3 engineers + 1 DS.

Phase 2 · Months 2–3
PPO + Environment Model

Shared PPO policy conditioned on agent profile z. Rule-based ABM environment for CPG, health, media domains. GLP-1/alcohol proof-of-concept with trajectory visualization. Team: 4–5 engineers + 2 DS.

Phase 3 · Months 4–6
Mean Field Multi-Agent

Aggregate behavioral statistics by segment as mean field μ. Policy conditions on (state, profile, μ). Social influence dynamics emerge naturally. Network structure optional. Team: 5–6 engineers + 2 DS + 1 researcher.

Phase 4 · Months 6+
Production Scale (100K+ Agents)

Learned transition models replace hand-designed rules. GPU-parallelized simulation. Client-facing scenario builder. Uncertainty quantification via ensemble policies. Automated calibration pipeline.

Key Design Decisions

Agent Profile (Frozen)

Causal VAE profiles are frozen during RL training. VAE trains on observational data; RL learns behavior in dynamic environments. Update one without retraining the other.

Reward Signal

Start with aggregate behavior matching: R = −DKL(Psim ∥ Pobs). Add individual likelihood as panel data becomes available. Most clients provide aggregate data first.

07 What I'd Build

Connecting the causal backbone to Lumen, Polls, and Seraph

Lumen (Synthetic Survey)

Replace the response generation layer with the Causal VAE. Agents respond through their causal structure, not correlation lookups. Run do(policy = X) on 10K agents and see the distribution of belief shifts — not just "will they like it?" but "how does it change their entire profile?"

Polls (Prediction)

Population-level RL simulation gives distributional predictions with confidence intervals, not point estimates. Scenario comparison via Thompson Sampling: "Should we target millennials or Gen X?" → simulate both → allocate compute to the more uncertain scenario → report with uncertainty.

Seraph (Agent Platform)

Each agent's causal profile becomes persistent state. The DAG provides explainability for every prediction. When a client asks "why did this agent change?" — trace the latent dimensions that shifted and map to human-readable constructs.

Strategic insight: Aaru doesn't need N systems for N domains. It needs one RL-based simulation engine with domain-specific environment plugins. Political modeling is an environment. CPG behavior is an environment. Health behavior is an environment. The agent architecture is universal. This is how a 24-person team stays efficient.
