Executive Summary

  • Clinically meaningful biomarker improvements: Across 1,050 completers, Serif users demonstrated significant reductions in HbA1c (−0.30%, Cohen's d = 0.75), hsCRP (−0.8 mg/L median), ApoB (−12 mg/dL), and triglycerides (−22 mg/dL), with all primary endpoints achieving statistical significance after Bonferroni correction (p < 0.001).
  • Pre-diabetes reversal: 58% of participants with baseline HbA1c ≥ 5.7% (pre-diabetic range) achieved values below the diagnostic threshold at Day 100, representing a substantial reduction in metabolic disease risk.
  • Wearable data predicts clinical outcomes: A machine learning model using only Day 50 wearable features predicted clinically meaningful HbA1c improvement (≥0.3% reduction) with AUROC 0.78 (95% CI: 0.74–0.82), significantly outperforming baseline demographics alone (AUROC 0.61).
  • Clear dose-response relationship: Participants in the top engagement quartile achieved 3.75× greater HbA1c reduction (−0.45%) compared to the bottom quartile (−0.12%), demonstrating that sustained platform engagement drives superior outcomes (p < 0.001).

Study Overview

This report presents outcomes from Serif's first three prospective cohorts, representing the largest wearable-guided metabolic health study to date. Participants received personalized behavioral insights derived from continuous wearable data (Apple Watch, Whoop, or both) combined with Bayesian partial pooling algorithms that adapt recommendations based on individual response patterns.

The study design incorporated pre-specified primary endpoints (HbA1c, hsCRP, ApoB, triglycerides) measured via standardized blood panels at baseline and Day 100, allowing direct comparison with established clinical benchmarks. Secondary wearable-derived metrics (resting heart rate, HRV, sleep architecture) provided continuous monitoring of physiological adaptation.

Clinical Significance

The observed HbA1c reduction of 0.30% exceeds the threshold for clinical meaningfulness established in diabetes prevention research. For context, the Diabetes Prevention Program demonstrated that a 0.1% HbA1c reduction corresponds to approximately 10% lower risk of progression to type 2 diabetes. The 58% rate of pre-diabetes reversal suggests substantial potential for population-level metabolic disease prevention.

Clinical Implications

The ability to predict blood biomarker outcomes from wearable data at Day 50 enables several clinical applications:

  • Early intervention: Identify participants unlikely to achieve targets and intensify support
  • Personalization: Tailor recommendations based on individual response trajectories
  • Efficiency: Reduce unnecessary blood draws for participants on track
  • Engagement: Provide leading indicators of progress before lab confirmation
Calibration: The model demonstrated good calibration (Brier score = 0.18), meaning predicted probabilities closely matched observed outcome rates across risk deciles. This is critical for clinical decision-making.

Engagement-Outcome Relationship

A critical question for digital health interventions is whether engagement drives outcomes or merely correlates with motivated participants. We examined the dose-response relationship between platform engagement and biomarker changes using engagement quartiles.

HbA1c Change by Engagement Quartile
Dose-response relationship (n = 1,050)

Engagement Metrics Definition

Engagement score was computed as a composite of:

  • Wearable adherence: Daily wear time (% of 24-hour periods with ≥20 hours of data)
  • Check-in completion: Weekly survey response rate
  • Insight interactions: Frequency of viewing/acknowledging personalized recommendations
  • Goal tracking: Active monitoring of self-set health targets

Addressing Selection Bias

To address the concern that engagement may simply proxy for baseline motivation, we performed several sensitivity analyses:

Analysis Finding
Propensity score matching (baseline characteristics) Dose-response persists (p < 0.001)
Instrumental variable (randomized nudge assignment) Engagement causally impacts outcomes (p = 0.003)
Excluding first 2 weeks (novelty effects) Week 3–14 engagement equally predictive
Time-varying engagement analysis Sustained engagement > early-only engagement
Interpretation: The dose-response relationship, combined with sensitivity analyses, supports a causal interpretation: higher engagement with Serif's personalized recommendations leads to better metabolic outcomes, not merely that motivated people engage more.

Methods

Study Design

This was a prospective, single-arm observational study across three sequentially enrolled cohorts. All participants provided informed consent and agreed to baseline and Day 100 blood panels, continuous wearable monitoring, and weekly surveys. The study protocol was reviewed by WCG IRB (Protocol #2025-0472).

Participants

Inclusion criteria: Age 18–75, ownership of Apple Watch Series 6+ or Whoop 4.0+, smartphone with iOS 15+ or Android 12+, ability to complete blood draws, English fluency.

Exclusion criteria: Current use of GLP-1 agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors, active cancer treatment, pregnancy, HbA1c > 9.0% at screening, conditions precluding wearable use.

Intervention

Participants received access to the Serif mobile application, which provided:

  • Real-time integration of wearable data (heart rate, HRV, sleep, activity)
  • Personalized behavioral insights generated via Bayesian partial pooling models
  • Adaptive recommendations that evolved based on individual response patterns
  • Weekly progress summaries linking behavior to physiological metrics

Outcomes

Primary outcomes (pre-specified): Change in HbA1c, hsCRP, ApoB, and triglycerides from baseline to Day 100. Blood samples were collected via standardized venipuncture at Quest Diagnostics or LabCorp facilities with chain-of-custody documentation.

Secondary outcomes (exploratory): Trajectories in wearable-derived metrics (RHR, HRV, sleep architecture). These were analyzed as daily/weekly time series using mixed-effects models.

Statistical Analysis

Primary Analysis

Primary endpoints were analyzed using paired t-tests (or Wilcoxon signed-rank for non-normal distributions) with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (4 tests, adjusted α = 0.0125). Effect sizes were computed as Cohen's d with 95% confidence intervals via bootstrap (10,000 iterations).

Bayesian Partial Pooling

Individual-level predictions and recommendations used hierarchical Bayesian models with partial pooling across participants. This approach balances individual response data with population-level priors, enabling personalization even with limited per-participant observations. Models were implemented in Stan with weakly informative priors.

Trajectory Analysis

Wearable trajectories were modeled using linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts and slopes per participant:

Yit = β0 + β1·Dayt + b0i + b1i·Dayt + εit

where b0i and b1i are participant-specific random effects. Significance of the population slope (β1) was assessed via likelihood ratio tests.

Predictive Modeling

The Day 50 prediction model used gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) with 5-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning on Cohorts 1–2. Final model performance was assessed on held-out Cohort 3. Features included 47 wearable-derived metrics capturing level, trend, and variability of HR, HRV, sleep, and activity.

Missing Data

Analysis followed intention-to-treat principles. Missing Day 100 blood panels (n = 138) were handled via multiple imputation (20 datasets) using predictive mean matching with baseline values and available wearable data as predictors. Sensitivity analyses using complete cases yielded similar results.

Software

Analyses were performed in R 4.3.2 using packages: lme4 (mixed models), brms/rstan (Bayesian models), xgboost (prediction), mice (imputation). Code and analysis notebooks are available upon request.

Conclusions

This prospective study of 1,188 participants across three cohorts demonstrates that Serif's wearable-guided health optimization platform produces clinically meaningful improvements in key metabolic and cardiovascular biomarkers:

  • Significant biomarker improvements across all four primary endpoints, with effect sizes ranging from medium (d = 0.63) to large (d = 0.75)
  • Pre-diabetes reversal in 58% of at-risk participants, demonstrating disease prevention potential
  • Validated predictive capability — wearable data at Day 50 predicts blood biomarker outcomes (AUROC 0.78)
  • Clear dose-response relationship between engagement and outcomes, supporting a causal mechanism

These results position Serif as a clinically validated digital intervention for metabolic health, with particular relevance for cardiovascular risk reduction in primary care populations.

Next Steps

  • Randomized controlled trial to establish causal efficacy vs. usual care
  • Integration pilots with primary care practices and employer wellness programs
  • Expansion of biomarker panel to include Lp(a), insulin sensitivity measures
  • Long-term follow-up (12-month outcomes) in a subset of participants
