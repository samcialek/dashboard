Key Finding: The Smoothness–Structure Tradeoff

β-VAE achieves 0.989 reconstruction by essentially memorizing inputs, but its latent space is smooth without causal structure. iter4d sacrifices reconstruction (0.909) to enforce DAG constraints, and that structure is what drives better downstream prediction (R² 0.202 vs 0.183). The gap suggests we need discrete structure, not continuous smoothness — which is exactly what VQ-VAE provides.

Negative Result: Hard Quantization Kills Signal

VQ-VAE’s best R² (0.143 at K=512) is 29% worse than iter4d (0.202). The hard argmin quantization destroys gradient information that the DAG structure preserves. Codebook usage collapses with scale: K=2048 uses only 1.7% of codes (34 of 2048). This is a known failure mode — the “codebook collapse” problem.

Path forward (4 options):

  1. Gumbel-Softmax — differentiable relaxation of discrete bottleneck
  2. Product quantization — factored codebook (fewer codes per subspace)
  3. VQ after DAG — quantize iter4d’s structured latent space, not raw encoder output
  4. Two-stage (fastest) — train iter4d, then cluster its latent space with k-means or CRP post-hoc

4. Migration Roadmap

Four phases from current causal VAE to distributed archetype simulation.

Phase 1: Causal VAE Proof

DAG-constrained latent space. Completed — iter4d achieves R² 0.202 with causal structure.

Phase 2: VQ-VAE Training — negative result

Hard quantization underperforms iter4d by 29%. Codebook collapse at high K. Pivoting to two-stage or Gumbel-Softmax approaches.

Phase 3: Population Calibration

π-weight estimation from census/survey data. Each archetype represents a known population fraction.

Phase 4: Distributed Deployment + CRP Dynamics

One node per archetype. Nonparametric type genesis via CRP. α dial for social diversity.
