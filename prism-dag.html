L1 — Innate Temperament

6 neurobiological primitives. Largely heritable, stable by late adolescence. The raw material that formative experience refracts into personality.

[F] — Formative Refraction MICRO + MACRO

Family, religion, trauma, education (micro) PLUS region, economic era, cultural zeitgeist, media environment (macro). Both shape how innate temperament crystallizes. Re-opens during phase transitions.

L2 — Crystallized Personality

6 dispositional traits. Each is f(2-4 L1 nodes) refracted through [F]. Durable but not immutable — phase transitions can re-open formative refraction.

[M] — Milieu MICRO + MACRO

Micro: personal life events (unobservable per-person, but base rates predictable in aggregate — you can't predict WHO gets divorced, but you can predict the divorce rate). Macro: recession, inflation, institutional decay (observable, same for all).

L3 — Political Disposition

14 nodes across 4 clusters. ENDS (what you want), MEANS (how you get there), REALITY (what you believe is true), SELF (who you are politically). 12 continuous (position 1-5 + salience) + 2 categorical (6 categories each). The inflection point: last layer before everything becomes macro.

[C] — Choice Architecture MACRO

Candidates, platforms, ballot access, mobilization, social cost, election cycle, scandal, crisis, media, zeitgeist. Purely macro — same for everyone in a given district. Fully observable and modelable. This is why L4 is more predictable than L3.

L4 — Political Behavior

Observable output. Turnout (binary), Vote Choice (party/candidate), Resistance-Compliance Scale (-3 to +3 for non-electoral behavior). NOT a lookup table — f(L3, practical constraints).

Predictability Gradient

L2→L3: LEAST predictable (micro + macro refraction, can't observe individual life events). L3→L4: MOST predictable (pure macro refraction, all observable). L3 is the inflection point — "closer to the bone."
