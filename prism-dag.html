L1 — Innate Temperament

6 neurobiological primitives. Largely heritable, stable by late adolescence. TR (Threat Reactivity), ND (Novelty Drive), EMP (Empathic Capacity), RX (Temperamental Reactance), AF (Affiliative Drive), CF (Cognitive Flexibility).

[F] — Formative Refraction

Family, region, religion, education, class, trauma, media environment. Refracts innate temperament into crystallized personality. Many-to-many mapping. Re-opens during phase transitions (divorce, war, migration).

L2 — Crystallized Personality

6 dispositional traits. Each is f(2-4 L1 nodes) × [F]. Traditionalism, Economic Conservatism, Compassion, Defiance, Gregariousness, Risk Comfort. Durable but not immutable.

[E] — Economic/Political Conditions

Both micro (personal life events) AND macro (recession, inflation, institutional decay). This makes L3 partly idiosyncratic — you can never fully observe individual circumstances.

L3 — Political Disposition

6 PRISM dimensions, each with position (0-100) and salience (0-1): MAT (Materialism), CSO (Current Social Order), CNA (Constitutional/National Authority), MOR (Moral Order), EPS (Epistemicism), PRO (Proceduralism). The inflection point: last layer before everything becomes macro.

[C] — Current Context

Election cycle, scandal, crisis, media environment. Activates salience on L3 nodes — doesn't change position, changes which positions matter right now (Overton window).

[P] — Political Architecture

Candidates, platforms, ballot access, mobilization infrastructure, social cost of participation. Purely macro — same for everyone in a given district. Fully observable.

L4 — Political Behavior

Observable output: TURNOUT (binary), VOTE (party choice), RCS (−3 to +3 resistance-compliance scale for non-electoral behavior). NOT a lookup table — f(L3, practical constraints).
