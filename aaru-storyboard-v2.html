Agent Construction
"How do you build a single synthetic person?"
What Exists

Joint Probability over Discrete State Space

Each agent is a point in a joint probability space P(X,Y) over 8^50 (~1045) configurations. Roughly 50 variables, each discretized into 8 levels. Bayes' rule enforced bidirectionally: P(Y|X) and P(X|Y) must both be consistent. Previously 8^300, collapsed down as a bias-variance decision.

The system is called Audience Gen. It defines ~103 personality and demographic traits per agent, all latent. Currently shipping "second generation tenement traits" (v2 trait schema). Matt Hauer is building a logical consistency replacement module to catch incoherent trait combinations ("embers" that keep sparking up after the main fire is out).

Three-stage pipeline per John Kessler: (1) classical ML agents (logistic regression, random forest) for structured predictions with known feature sets, (2) the joint probability model P(X,Y) for coherent population-level inference, (3) LLM-as-agent with persona cards for open-ended qualitative queries. Each stage trades off speed, interpretability, and expressiveness. An Agent SDK is under development for external developers.

This approach is closer to statistical mechanics (partition functions over discrete states) than traditional ABM. The "agents" aren't rule-based entities in a simulation; they're samples from a massive discrete probability space, and "simulation" means sampling trajectories consistent with the joint distribution.

Optimal

Hierarchical Bayesian Agents

State-of-the-art synthetic agents represent each person as an individual posterior distribution drawn from a population prior via partial pooling. Parameters are distributions, not point estimates. Uncertainty is first-class.

The literature (Gelman et al., BDA3; synthetic population microsimulation) shows that the key problems with discrete state-space approaches are: (1) curse of dimensionality makes enumeration impossible past ~20 variables, requiring compressed traversal; (2) discretization artifacts lose information at bin boundaries; (3) no principled uncertainty on the agent parameters themselves.

  • Population hyperprior → subgroup prior → individual posterior
  • MCMC/VI inference (NUTS, SVI, normalizing flows)
  • Automatic relevance determination for variable selection
  • Gaussian copulas or vine copulas for dependency structure
  • Variational autoencoders for learned latent representations
Sam's Edge

Probabilistic Programming (NumPyro + NUTS)

18 months production experience building hierarchical Bayesian models in NumPyro at Serif. Individual posteriors from cohort priors. Partial pooling is the native architecture: cohort-level distributions narrow as individual data accumulates, with recommendations exposed only after sufficient posterior concentration.

  • Replaces heuristic state-space traversal with principled MCMC/VI
  • Model comparison via WAIC/LOO-CV (Vehtari et al.)
  • Prior sensitivity analysis and prior predictive checks
  • Composable generative models: swap likelihoods, add hierarchy
  • Production-tested: health outcome distributions for B2B SaaS clients
→ Direct plug-in to Aaru's P(X,Y) framework. Replaces heuristic traversal of 8^50 space with formal posterior inference over the populated regions.

Population Architecture
"How do you build a million agents that look like a real population?"
What Exists

700-Variable Differentially Private Synthetic Data

Aaru generates differentially private synthetic populations across 700 variables in seconds. For context, NVIDIA's persona product uses six variables. The US Census Bureau spent $100M on TopDown to achieve something similar (and arguably worse). This is the most technically impressive confirmed capability.

Population assembly likely uses iterative proportional fitting (IPF/raking) or a generative model to match known marginal distributions from census, ACS, and survey data, then fills in the joint distribution. The 103 personality traits are assigned as conditional draws from the joint model.

Core philosophy: no archetypes, no clustering. Every individual modeled with full intersecting identities. Hauer explicitly rejects reducing people to types because "the real world is messiness."

Optimal

Deep Generative Population Synthesis

State-of-the-art synthetic population generation uses deep generative models: VAEs, normalizing flows (RealNVP, Glow), diffusion models, or vine copulas. These learn the full joint distribution over continuous and discrete variables without discretization.

Key advances beyond IPF/raking: (1) CTGAN / TVAE (Xu et al., 2019) for tabular data synthesis; (2) normalizing flows that give exact log-likelihoods; (3) Bayesian nonparametric approaches (Dirichlet process mixtures) that automatically determine the number of subpopulations.

  • Preserves all pairwise and higher-order correlations
  • Conditional generation: fix known marginals, sample the rest
  • Privacy: formal DP-SGD guarantees during training (Abadi et al.)
  • Evaluation: statistical fidelity (JSD, MMD), ML efficacy, privacy leakage (membership inference)
  • Multi-resolution: national → state → county → individual with consistent marginals
Sam's Edge

Partial Pooling + Sparse Subgroup Handling

Hierarchical model where agent parameters are drawn from subgroup-level distributions. Archetypes as cluster centers, individuals as draws. Soft membership avoids Hauer's objection to hard clustering while still borrowing strength across similar agents.

Critical for Aaru's scale: with 700 variables, most subgroup intersections have near-zero observations. Partial pooling naturally regularizes toward the group mean when data is sparse, and toward the individual when data is rich.

  • Handles sparse subgroups without ad-hoc imputation
  • Uncertainty widens for rare populations (honest about what you don't know)
  • No hard clustering: compatible with Aaru's anti-archetype philosophy
  • Tested at Serif: cohort priors → individual posteriors for health outcomes
→ Bridges Aaru's anti-archetype philosophy with the practical need to handle sparse subpopulations in 700-dimensional space.

Behavior Simulation
"Given an agent with traits, how do you predict what they do?"
What Exists

Three-Model Ensemble with Known Gaps

Behavior is predicted by conditioning the joint P(X,Y): given traits X, derive likely behavior Y. The inverse P(X|Y) is also maintained for coherence checking. The LLM handles open-ended qualitative queries. Classical ML handles structured predictions with known feature sets.

Internal tension (Yoonduk): black-box neural nets yield better F1 scores, but interpretable trait variables offer explanatory and prescriptive power. "Do we just really want to predict the next thing really well?" This is unresolved.

Three confirmed gaps: (1) No counterfactual simulation: can't ask "what would this person have done if X were different?" (2) No abductive inference: can't reason backward from an outcome Y to what conditions X would have produced it. (3) Cyclic dependencies between traits break topological sort, creating computational headaches ("everything eating up into each other").

Optimal

Structural Causal Models + Pearl's Ladder

State-of-the-art behavioral simulation uses structural causal models (SCMs) that encode directed causal relationships. Pearl's Ladder of Causation (2018) defines three levels: association (observational), intervention (do-calculus), and counterfactual (individual-level "what if").

Key methods: (1) do-calculus for identifiability of causal effects from observational data; (2) twin networks for counterfactual reasoning; (3) abductive-action-prediction framework for computing counterfactuals via SCMs; (4) causal discovery algorithms (PC, GES, NOTEARS) to learn DAG structure from data.

  • Resolves correlation vs. causation: P(Y|X) ≠ P(Y|do(X))
  • Counterfactuals: "What would have happened if this person had seen ad B instead of ad A?"
  • Abduction: "Given this election result, what population conditions would explain it?"
  • Transportability: apply causal effects learned in one population to another
Sam's Edge

Causal DAGs + Abductive Inference

PRISM's layered DAG (Personality → Values → Formative → Disposition → Behavior, modulated by Milieu and Choice Architecture) implements causal structure. Enables do-calculus for interventional queries and abductive reasoning for reverse inference.

Crucially, resolves Yoonduk's cyclic dependency problem: apparent cycles are broken by time-indexing. A variable can cause itself at a later time, but within a time slice the graph is directed. This is standard in dynamic Bayesian networks (DBNs).

  • Fills gap: Counterfactual simulation via twin-network SCM approach
  • Fills gap: Abductive inference (outcome → best-explanation conditions)
  • Fills gap: Cycles → DAG with explicit temporal unrolling
  • Causal robustness framework (Matt wants to co-publish, doesn't reveal core IP)
  • Tested: causal confounders in health (rain → exercise → sleep example from Serif)
→ Sam's causal DAGs fill both confirmed gaps and resolve the cyclic dependency problem. The causal robustness framework is a publishable contribution that builds Aaru's academic credibility.

"What world do the agents live in, and how do you define it?"
What Exists

Clients define a scenario (election, marketing campaign, ICE raid sensitivity, pricing change) and agents respond to it. The environment is a fixed prompt or parameter set with no feedback loops, no agent-agent interaction, no temporal unfolding.

Optimal

Dynamic Multi-Agent Environments

State-of-the-art ABM (Epstein, Axtell; MASON; Mesa) models environments as endogenous stochastic processes that agents interact with and modify. Key paradigms:

  • Schelling models: simple local rules → emergent macro patterns (segregation, tipping)
  • Network contagion: information/behavior spreads through social graphs (Watts-Strogatz, Barabasi-Albert)
  • Multi-agent RL: agents learn policies that adapt to other agents' strategies (MARL, Nash equilibria)
  • Scenario trees: branch on key uncertainties, compute expected values across branches
  • Sensitivity analysis: Sobol indices, Morris screening to identify which parameters drive output variance
  • Game-theoretic: adversarial simulation as competitive equilibrium (Stackelberg, mechanism design)
Sam's Edge

Refraction Layers + Principled Scenario Reduction

PRISM models environment as refraction layers that mediate between disposition and behavior: [F] Formative (upbringing, culture), [M] Milieu (current economic/social context), [CA] Choice Architecture (what options are presented).

For the butterfly effect problem: apply Sobol sensitivity analysis or PCA on the scenario parameter space to identify the 3 to 5 axes that explain most output variance. Simulate on those axes instead of random context permutations. Gives clients an honest "these are the parameters that matter most for your question."

  • Separates endogenous (agent) from exogenous (world) in a principled way
  • Scenario reduction: Sobol + PCA to find dominant context dimensions
  • Nash equilibrium framing for adversarial simulation (competitor modeling)
  • Directly maps to Aaru's client deliverable: "here's what happens under these 5 scenarios"
→ Addresses the butterfly effect with formal sensitivity analysis. Turns ad-hoc scenario definition into principled scenario reduction.

Optimal

Multi-Level Validation Pipeline

Best practice for simulation validation operates at four levels (Kennedy & O'Hagan, 2001; Bayarri et al.):

  • Internal consistency: trait coherence checks (no impossible trait bundles). Gelman's "p-value" style checks for model misfit.
  • Posterior predictive checks (PPC): simulate data from model, compare distributional shape to real data. Not just point accuracy but full distribution match.
  • External validation: holdout sets, cross-domain transfer (e.g., model trained on 2020 predicts 2022 midterms). Out-of-sample calibration curves.
  • Individual plausibility: sample agents and assess whether they represent real-world-plausible human profiles. Subject matter expert review + automated scoring.
  • Model comparison: WAIC, LOO-CV, Bayes factors for comparing model variants. Stacking weights for ensemble models.
  • Sensitivity: how do outputs change as priors or hyperparameters vary?
Sam's Edge

Bayesian Model Checking Pipeline

Standard Bayesian workflow (Gelman et al., Bayesian Data Analysis 3e): posterior predictive checks, individual plausibility scoring, WAIC/LOO model comparison, prior sensitivity analysis. All standard tools in NumPyro/Stan/brms.

Key insight for the latent trait problem: even if you can't validate traits directly, you can validate their downstream observable predictions. If agents with inferred trait profiles produce accurate behavioral predictions, the traits are justified instrumentally. This is the latent → observable validation chain.

  • Automates ember-fixing: PPC flags incoherent agents automatically instead of Matt checking them one by one

  • Model comparison at scale: swap likelihoods, compare WAIC scores, identify which model variants work for which domains

Temporal Dynamics & Decision Theory
"How do agents change over time, and how do you use simulations to make decisions?"
What Exists

Snapshot Simulation with Markov Checkpointing

Generate a population at a point in time, simulate one event, deliver results. Clients mostly want cross-sectional snapshots. When temporal reasoning is needed, the Markov assumption is applied: "imagine everything before is represented in the current state."

Known limitations (Yoonduk): "Decisions from 20 years ago might seem to [matter] a year later." The Markov assumption drops long-range dependencies that matter for things like radicalization, brand loyalty decay, and voter realignment. Yoonduk explicitly wants the ability to "roll things back, change things, and roll forward."

No decision-theoretic output: Simulations produce point predictions or distributions, but there's no framework for converting those into optimal decisions under uncertainty. Clients get "here's what will happen" but not "here's what you should do given your risk tolerance."

⚡ Yoonduk: "Decisions from 20 years ago might matter." Wants rollback/rollforward.
⚡ Hauer: "Most of the time clients don't want uncertainty." Georgia governor wanted exact numbers.
⚡ John: exploring "new modalities of simulation for past population" backtesting.
Optimal

Dynamic Bayesian Networks + Decision Theory

State-of-the-art temporal simulation uses dynamic Bayesian networks (DBNs) or state-space models with sequential Monte Carlo (particle filtering) for online inference. For decision-making:

  • Influence diagrams: extend Bayesian networks with decision and utility nodes
  • Expected utility maximization: optimal actions given posterior beliefs
  • Robust optimization: worst-case over uncertainty sets (distributionally robust)
  • CVaR optimization: expected value in the worst α% of outcomes
  • Multi-objective: Pareto frontiers when clients care about multiple outcomes
  • Information value: expected value of perfect/imperfect information (should client gather more data?)
Sam's Edge

Phase Transitions + CVaR Decision Theory

PRISM insight: most of life is a feed-forward DAG (steady state). But phase transitions (divorce, war, migration, recession) re-open formative layers, re-refracting personality through new circumstances. This is more nuanced than simple Markov: the posterior distribution itself encodes history without storing explicit trajectories.

CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk): Instead of optimizing for the expected outcome, optimize for the expected outcome in the worst tail. Client chooses risk posture → simulation output changes accordingly. "What's the best marketing strategy if we optimize for the worst 10% of scenarios?" This is the "Geometry of Preference" idea: political disagreements (and business strategy disagreements) are often just different distributional preferences.

  • Phase transitions: steady-state DAG ↔ RL exploration when conditions rupture
  • CVaR: tail-risk optimization for risk-averse clients
  • Geometry of Preference: different stakeholders want different quantiles
  • Hauer explicitly said they don't do distributional optimization yet, and loved the pitch
