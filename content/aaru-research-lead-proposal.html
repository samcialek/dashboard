Why This Role, Why Me

1. Causal Inference + Population Simulation

PRISM is a 71-archetype political disposition model built from 258K survey responses across 5 national datasets (ANES, GSS, Pew, CES, CCES). It operates across four layers:

  • L1 (Demographics) -- structural priors: age, education, income, geography
  • L2 (Psychographics) -- latent dispositions: moral foundations, risk tolerance, institutional trust
  • L3 (Issue Positions) -- derived from L1+L2 interactions via causal DAGs
  • L4 (Temporal Dynamics) -- exogenous shocks, drift, generational replacement

This maps directly to Aaru's agent architecture: archetypes are cluster centers, L4 is temporal dynamics, and the exogenous/endogenous function separation is exactly how you'd structure agent decision-making under external shocks.

2. The Causal VAE -- Mechanism Over Correlation

I built a DAG-constrained variational autoencoder that enforces causal structure in latent space. The key result: R²=0.202 predicting psychographic dispositions from demographics alone -- not by memorizing correlations, but by learning the generative process. When VQ-VAE failed (a genuine negative result), I pivoted to two-stage clustering and discovered natural archetypes that the model hadn't been told to find.

This is the kind of methodology Aaru needs at scale: models that degrade gracefully when the world shifts, because they've learned why, not just what.

3. The GLP-1 Case Study -- Simulation in Action

Through Serif, I built the mediated causal chain: Treatment → BMI → Confidence → Sociality → Alcohol. XGBoost sees that GLP-1 patients drink less. The causal model sees why: weight loss increases confidence, which changes social behavior, which reduces alcohol consumption. This is exactly the kind of analysis Aaru pitched on Squawk Box -- and exactly what a Research Lead should be building at scale across every domain Aaru touches.

  • End-to-end pipeline: data harmonization (258K rows, 5 surveys) → model training → evaluation → production interactive
  • Python/PyTorch, Bayesian methods (Stan, PyMC), DAG learning, causal inference
